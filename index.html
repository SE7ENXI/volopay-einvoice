3. Architecture

  • Data flow: UI form, then backend validation, then mapping to UBL, then partner API, then LHDN clearance. Status and errors come back by webhook to the backend and then to the user.
  • Where validation happens: (1) Volopay, instantly, for required fields, TIN pattern, RM10,000 individual-invoice rule and foreign currency rate. (2) LHDN, after submission, for registry checks such as whether a buyer TIN exists. Prototype simulates the second.

4. Built vs simulated, partner split, next steps

  • Actually works: form, local validation, totals, status table, rejection and fix loop, cancel within the 72-hour window rule, this diagram.
  • Simulated: Storecove submission, LHDN response, UUID, delay, database (lost on refresh), ERP sync.
  • Volopay builds: invoice data model, validation rules, approvals, AP matching, payments, ERP sync, UX. Storecove provides: UBL conversion, MyInvois connectivity, status webhooks, so Volopay avoids building a direct LHDN integration.
  • Next steps to launch: open Storecove sandbox, map full 55-field LHDN data set, replace TIN rule with registry check, add multi-line and consolidated invoices, security review, pilot with 5 customers.
  • Assumptions: Malaysian rules (RM10,000 individual invoice threshold, 72-hour cancellation, foreign currency rate) come from public guides and must be re-checked against LHDN before production.
